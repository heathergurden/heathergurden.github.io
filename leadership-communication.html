Best Practices for Leadership Communication

A 15-minute presentation for an integrated project team that pairs four foundational leadership behaviors with practical communication tools to prevent the leading cause of project failure.

At a Glance

Problem

Poor communication is the leading cause of project failure, and integrated project teams bring together people from different departments and levels of authority.

Approach

I designed a presentation that pairs four leadership behaviors (respect, listening first, clear expectations, and accountability) with the tools teams use to keep information flowing.

Result

A deck with full speaker notes that reframes project success as a communication problem and closes with four takeaways the team can act on.

Design Rationale

Key Decisions

01

Opening with the stakes

I started with a blunt hook, “Your project failed,” and a PMI statistic so the audience understood the cost before hearing any advice. I framed each behavior as a tool for anyone, “whether you are of formal or informal authority,” so the message landed at every level.

02

Treating technology as support, not the solution

Many teams expect a new tool to fix a struggling project. The deck argues that results depend on how people communicate, then maps a team toolkit and recommends choosing tools by team needs and project complexity.

03

Letting the speaker carry the detail

Each slide holds one behavior in short action phrases, supported by visual metaphors like a seedling for communication and a Newton’s cradle for momentum. The speaker notes carry the full argument, so the audience listens instead of reading ahead.

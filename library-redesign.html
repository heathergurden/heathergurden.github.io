My Work Library Homepage Redesign

UX Case Study

Library Homepage Redesign

A redesign of mld.org that follows the process from discovery research through usability testing, with the goal of making the homepage easier to use for long-time patrons, first-time visitors, and people with limited web experience.

UX Research • Usability Testing • Prototyping • Information Architecture

At a Glance

Problem

The Meridian Library District homepage felt outdated, overly colorful, and hard to scan for the information patrons need most.

Approach

Over five sprints, I surveyed 11 library users, ran a heuristic evaluation, built a Figma prototype, and moderated usability tests with participants ranging from a regular patron to an 8-year-old.

Result

Every participant completed every task, and the youngest tester's feedback led to a clearer dropdown arrow and plain-language labels in the final design.

Design Rationale

Key Decisions

01

Designing for the least experienced user

An 8-year-old tester didn't recognize the dropdown arrow, even though every participant finished every task. Instead of calling the design done, I made the arrow larger and higher in contrast so it works for anyone new to the web.

02

Putting patrons' top tasks first

Survey data showed catalog search, events, and hours drove most visits, so they became the hero search bar and the two panels directly below it.

03

Keeping familiar patterns, adding clear labels

I kept conventions like a top navigation bar and a "My Account" button because users rely on them. Testing showed those patterns are learned, not universal, so the final design pairs every icon with a text label.

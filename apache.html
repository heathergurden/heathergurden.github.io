At a Glance

Problem

East Valley developers wanted Apache Junction, Arizona residents, businesses, and schools to reduce water use by 5%, without messaging that feels like a restriction.

Approach

As a media consultant, I researched the city's existing conservation program and communication channels, then built a two-page plan of key messages, tactics, channels, and evaluation measures.

Result

A campaign that frames conservation as a shared responsibility and can launch on channels the city already runs.

Strategy Rationale

Key Decisions

01

Framing conservation as shared ownership

Scarcity and blame messages can make people defensive. Instead, the key messages tell residents the community's future is in their hands and promote the city's own "One Water: Our Water" program in its own language.

02

Six audiences, six strategies

A golf course owner and a tourist need different reasons to save water. I split "the public" into six audiences, each with its own messenger, channel, and frequency, and paired compliance standards with tax incentives for businesses.

03

Building on what the city already had

Every channel in the plan, from AJ Cable Channel 11 to the Citizen Leadership Institute, already exists, so the campaign needs no new systems. The full audience strategy sits in an appendix, keeping the plan readable for busy decision makers.

At a Glance

Problem

Davies Pearson, a fictional literature professor with a private collection of rare books, needed a web presence that earns a collector’s trust before they ever ask about a book.

Approach

I developed a website proposal with homepage and interior page prototypes, a scope plan, a business narrative, a style guide, and a testing and maintenance plan.

Result

A site designed for credibility rather than inventory, ready for a developer to build exactly as designed.

Strategy Rationale

Key Decisions

01

Designing for trust, not sales

Collectors are cautious and need to trust a source before they act. I organized the site around three paths (judge Davies’s credibility, browse the collection, and request a service), and every page ends with the same invitation to contact him.

02

Leaving features out on purpose

I cut e-commerce, a scanned digital library, and a blog. Davies works through relationships, not a shopping cart, and has little time for upkeep, so technology earns its place only where it helps, like a catalog that updates availability automatically.

03

Planning to prove it and keep it current

The proposal includes moderated task testing and a first-click test with a 70 percent accuracy target, plus a maintenance plan with a clear owner. Drawing on my experience with intranet governance, I designed the site to stay accurate long after launch.
